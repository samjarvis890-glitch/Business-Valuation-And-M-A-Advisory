ADVISORY PRACTICE 01

Business Valuation Methodologies & Analysis

Comprehensive answers addressing how privately held enterprises, operating assets, and shareholdings are objectively evaluated under internationally recognized valuation principles.

Independent Valuation Frameworks

Our valuation opinions apply rigorous income, market, and asset-based methodologies to withstand transaction negotiations, tax evaluations, and judicial review.

Explore Valuation Services

Valuation methodology selection is governed by the company’s operating profile, earnings stability, capital structure, and the underlying purpose of the valuation. At Northbridge, we typically triangulate value across three primary disciplines:

  • Income Approach (Discounted Cash Flow): Applied when a business demonstrates predictable multi-year free cash flows or distinct growth trajectories, discounting future cash flows at a risk-adjusted Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC).
  • Market Approach (Comparable Multiples): Utilized for businesses with established operating benchmarks, benchmarking Enterprise Value against guideline public companies and recent precedent M&A transactions across EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, or EV/Revenue.
  • Asset-Based Approach: Employed primarily for asset-heavy enterprises, holding companies, early-stage operations, or situations where orderly liquidation value represents the economic floor.

In practice, our concluded valuation synthesizes multiple methodologies, applying weighted significance based on data reliability and industry norms.

Understanding the bridge between Enterprise Value and Equity Value is essential for both buyers and sellers:

Enterprise Value (EV) represents the total economic value of the operating entity as a whole, independent of its capital structure. It reflects the core operational enterprise accessible to all capital providers (both debt holders and equity shareholders).

Equity Value represents the net value attributable specifically to equity shareholders after accounting for the company’s net debt position.

Equity Value = Enterprise Value − Total Debt + Cash & Cash Equivalents + Non-Operating Assets − Minority Interests

Most M&A offers are expressed on a "cash-free, debt-free" basis at the Enterprise Value level, which is subsequently adjusted for working capital and net cash at financial closing.

To ensure analytical rigor and defensibility, our valuation team typically reviews the following documentation:

  • Three to five years of historical audited, reviewed, or management financial statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement).
  • Year-to-date interim financial statements alongside corresponding prior-year comparative periods.
  • Detailed breakdown of owner/director compensation, non-operating expenses, and discretionary personal items.
  • Management financial forecasts, operating budgets, and capital expenditure schedules.
  • Customer concentration reports, supplier dependency schedules, and employee headcount breakdowns.
  • Current capitalization table, debt instruments, lease commitments, and shareholder agreements.

All sensitive documentation is received under a binding Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) and housed within secure, encrypted data repositories.

Reported financial statements of privately owned businesses frequently reflect tax-minimization strategies, non-recurring expenses, or shareholder-specific costs that do not represent true ongoing operational earning power.

Earnings normalisation (calculating Adjusted or Normalized EBITDA) bridges reported accounting numbers to repeatable cash-generating capability by adjusting for:

  • Excess or below-market owner remuneration benchmarked to commercial executive replacement costs.
  • One-off professional fees (litigation settlements, M&A setup costs, restructuring charges).
  • Related-party property leases adjusted to independent fair market rent.
  • Non-operating assets, discretionary travel, or non-commercial corporate overhead.

Because valuation multiples apply directly to Adjusted EBITDA, an adjustment of \$100,000 at a 6.0x multiple creates a \$600,000 variance in implied Enterprise Value.

Business valuation combines scientific financial mathematics with qualitative professional judgment. Differences between professional opinions typically stem from distinct analytical inputs:

  • Discount Rate Assumptions: Variations in company-specific risk premiums, size premiums, or cost of equity determinations within the WACC framework.
  • Earnings Normalisation: Differing treatment of non-recurring expenses, owner remuneration levels, and sustainable working capital requirements.
  • Peer Group Selection: Differing criteria applied when filtering comparable public entities or precedent M&A transactions.
  • Growth & Margin Trajectories: Divergent perspectives on long-term terminal growth rates and margin sustainability amidst competitive headwinds.

Northbridge produces transparent sensitivity tables alongside concluded valuation ranges, demonstrating explicitly how key inputs alter value outcomes.

Yes. Northbridge frequently serves as an independent, unbiased valuation advisor in shareholder transactions, partner exits, corporate restructuring, and commercial dispute resolutions.

In minority interest situations, we provide specialized analysis of applicable valuation discounts, including:

  • Discount for Lack of Control (DLOC): Reflecting the reduced economic sovereignty associated with non-controlling equity positions.
  • Discount for Lack of Marketability (DLOM): Quantifying the liquidity impairment inherent in unlisted private equity securities compared to public equities.

Our reports are structured to withstand cross-examination, tax authority scrutiny, and legal arbitrations.

A standard comprehensive valuation engagement typically requires three to five weeks from receipt of complete financial information:

  • Weeks 1–2: Information intake, initial data verification, management diagnostic interview, and normalisation schedule formulation.
  • Weeks 2–3: Financial modeling, DCF formulation, comparable company and precedent transaction multiple benchmarking.
  • Weeks 3–4: Senior partner peer review, sensitivity testing, draft valuation report assembly, and preliminary findings discussion.
  • Week 5: Final deliverable finalization, executive presentation, and delivery of formal signed valuation memorandum.

Expedited turnaround timelines are available for time-sensitive transactions or pending regulatory deadlines.

ADVISORY PRACTICE 02

Mergers, Acquisitions & Transaction Strategy

Strategic answers for business founders, corporate boards, and acquisition teams preparing for corporate divestitures, acquisitions, and confidential negotiations.

An M&A advisor guides business owners through the entire transaction lifecycle to maximize proceeds, protect deal terms, and manage transaction risks:

  • Evaluating baseline transaction readiness and establishing realistic valuation parameters.
  • Preparing institutional-grade marketing materials (Blind Profile Teaser and Confidential Information Memorandum).
  • Conducting targeted buyer identification across strategic acquirers and private equity sponsors.
  • Structuring a competitive bidding process to create negotiation leverage.
  • Coordinating confirmatory due diligence within secure virtual data rooms (VDR).
  • Negotiating commercial purchase terms, working capital pegs, and indemnification caps alongside legal counsel.

Optimal exit planning should ideally begin 12 to 24 months prior to entering the market. Advance preparation allows shareholders to:

  • Remediate customer or supplier concentration vulnerabilities.
  • Transition critical client relationships and operational dependencies away from owner-operators to secondary leadership.
  • Clean up historical accounting records and transition to audited or reviewed statements.
  • Document internal operating systems, intellectual property rights, and commercial employment agreements.
  • Demonstrate consistent financial margin expansion rather than attempting to justify speculative future projections.

We construct a bespoke buyer universe tailored to the company's size, sector, and strategic strengths, categorizing targets into:

  • Strategic Acquirers: Industry competitors, complementary service providers, or vertical supply chain partners capable of achieving operational synergies.
  • Financial Sponsors: Private equity funds, family offices, and independent sponsors with active investment theses and available platform capital.

Every prospect is screened for financial capacity, transaction track record, and cultural compatibility before receiving initial anonymous marketing teasers.

A Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM), also known as an Information Memorandum, is the definitive comprehensive marketing document provided to qualified acquirers after execution of an NDA.

A Northbridge CIM provides a detailed, institutional-level narrative covering:

  • Executive summary and core investment thesis highlights.
  • Industry dynamics, addressable market scale, and competitive positioning.
  • Detailed products, services, operational infrastructure, and technology stack.
  • Detailed financial analysis, historical normalized EBITDA bridges, and forecast models.
  • Organizational hierarchy, key personnel, and post-transaction governance plans.

A headline transaction valuation is only as valuable as the structural terms governing payment. Common consideration components include:

  • Cash at Closing: Guaranteed proceeds received at settlement, representing risk-free liquidity.
  • Vendor Note (Seller Financing): A deferred loan from seller to buyer, bearing interest over a 2–5 year term; provides yields but carries subordination risk.
  • Earn-Out: Contingent payments tied to post-closing EBITDA or gross revenue milestones; bridges valuation expectations but demands strict operational controls.
  • Rollover Equity: Retaining 10%–30% minority interest in the recapitalized entity, offering upside during a subsequent institutional secondary exit.

Our advisors model every deal structure on a risk-adjusted after-tax basis before negotiating final terms.

Uncontrolled market rumors can disrupt client relationships, destabilize employees, and alert competitors. We protect confidentiality through rigid protocols:

  • Initial market outreach is strictly conducted on a "no-name" anonymous basis using an anonymized One-Page Executive Teaser.
  • The identity of the business is only disclosed after prospective buyers sign a customized Non-Disclosure Agreement pre-approved by the seller.
  • Granular data, contracts, and customer names are staged within encrypted Virtual Data Rooms with watermarking and access restrictions.

ADVISORY PRACTICE 03

Financial Due Diligence & Quality of Earnings

Analytical clarity on pre-transaction verification, accounting reconciliations, working capital targets, and validating cash flow sustainability.

While a statutory audit verifies historical compliance with GAAP or IFRS accounting standards, a Quality of Earnings review is specifically engineered to evaluate the sustainability, quality, and cash conversion of operating earnings for transaction purposes:

  • An audit confirms whether past balances are materially correct; a QofE assesses whether historical earnings can realistically be expected to recur in future periods.
  • QofE dissects customer and product-level gross margins, monthly revenue seasonality, and cash collection cycles.
  • QofE identifies run-rate adjustments (e.g. annualized impact of mid-year contract wins or price adjustments) that statutory audits do not evaluate.

In transactions executed on a "debt-free, cash-free" basis, the buyer expects to receive a sufficient level of Net Working Capital (NWC) at closing to operate the business uninterrupted.

The Working Capital Peg (or Target) is the agreed baseline level of normalized working capital established during negotiations (typically based on a trailing 12-month average). At closing:

  • If closing NWC exceeds the agreed peg, the purchase price is adjusted upward in favor of the seller.
  • If closing NWC falls below the agreed peg, the buyer receives a downward purchase price adjustment or escrow release.

Our due diligence team performs trailing monthly analyses to prevent seasonal cash distortions from eroding seller equity value.

When a single customer accounts for more than 15% to 20% of total revenue, buyers and lenders perceive heightened cash flow vulnerability.

During due diligence, we evaluate contract duration, historical order consistency, switching costs, and customer relationship tenure. To mitigate buyer concerns, deals involving high concentration often incorporate structured milestone earn-outs, escrow holdbacks, or vendor financing tied to client retention.

Yes. Sell-side vendor due diligence (VDD) is one of the highest ROI investments a business owner can make prior to transaction launch.

By commissioning an independent quality of earnings review upfront, sellers can proactively uncover accounting inconsistencies, formalize defensible EBITDA adjustments, verify revenue recognition policies, and eliminate unexpected buyer re-trading during exclusivity.

Transactions rarely fail due to commercial disagreement; they typically stall when confirmatory diligence reveals unaddressed operational or accounting discrepancies:

  • Unreconciled revenue recognition practices between cash and accrual accounting.
  • Unfunded employee leave entitlements or contingent liabilities omitted from management accounts.
  • Sudden deterioration in gross margin performance during the final pre-closing months.
  • Incomplete documentation of proprietary intellectual property or software ownership.
  • Failure to agree on normalized working capital definitions prior to final legal agreements.

ADVISORY PRACTICE 04

Financial Modelling, Forecasting & Strategic Sensitivity

Practical explanations on dynamic three-statement models, debt service coverage covenants, downside scenario tables, and institutional forecasting standards.

Simple management budgets typically isolate profit and loss forecasts without mathematically connecting them to working capital movements, debt servicing, and cash flow generation.

An institutional-grade three-statement financial model fully integrates the Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement through dynamic formulaic links:

  • Net Income automatically flows into Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet and anchors the operating cash flow statement.
  • Working capital requirements (DSO, DIO, DPO) dynamically determine cash consumption as sales fluctuate.
  • Capital expenditures update fixed asset depreciation schedules and book balances.
  • Cash surplus or shortfall drives automated debt sweeps and interest expense calculations.

Transactions rarely fail under base-case conditions; boards need to understand capital resilience under severe downside shocks.

We construct automated sensitivity matrices evaluating two-variable combinations (e.g. revenue contraction vs. raw material cost inflation) to highlight breakeven thresholds, liquidity stress points, and debt covenant headroom before closing.

Yes. All financial models built by Northbridge are delivered as fully unlocked, transparent client assets with modular input schedules.

Executive management and board directors routinely utilize our models post-closing as rolling 12-month operational forecasting tools, tracking monthly actuals against acquisition investment theses.

Commercial lenders typically mandate a minimum Debt Service Coverage Ratio (e.g. 1.25x to 1.50x) alongside maximum senior leverage covenants (e.g. 2.5x to 3.0x EBITDA).

Our debt modeling incorporates detailed amortization schedules, cash flow sweeps, benchmark interest rate fluctuations, and simulated operational margin compressions to establish safe borrowing capacity.

We follow international financial modeling best practices (such as the FAST standard): strict separation of assumptions, calculations, and financial statement outputs; standardized font color coding (blue for hardcoded inputs, black for formulas, green for external links); elimination of circular references; and comprehensive internal balance checks.

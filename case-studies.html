CASE / 02 ILLUSTRATIVE ACQUISITION

Evaluating a Potential Business Acquisition.

A hypothetical organisation is exploring the acquisition of another business. Before progressing, decision-makers want to understand the financial information and wider business considerations involved.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

What should be understood before evaluating a potential acquisition?

CHALLENGE 01

Financial Understanding

Reviewing the available financial information, historical margins and performance trends.

CHALLENGE 02

Business Fit

Considering strategic objectives, operational integration and business model compatibility.

CHALLENGE 03

Risk Awareness

Identifying operational bottlenecks, key person dependencies and areas requiring further diligence.

CHALLENGE 04

Transaction Assumptions

Testing working capital requirements, debt capacity and assumptions relevant to the proposed transaction.

BUY-SIDE PERSPECTIVE

Strategic Valuation & Terms

  • • Financial performance: Historical EBITDA sustainability and normalized run-rates.
  • • Operating structure: Supply chain dependencies and capacity scalability.
  • • Strategic objectives: Synergies realization and commercial alignment.
  • • Financial obligations: Debt-like items, off-balance liabilities and deferred capex.
  • • Potential transaction risks: Customer concentration and contract renewal terms.
BUSINESS REVIEW PERSPECTIVE

Evidence & Diligence Verification

  • • Available financial information: Granularity and consistency of underlying books.
  • • Business operations: Management reporting systems and operational workflows.
  • • Relevant dependencies: Proprietary software, key accounts and supplier agreements.
  • • Supporting documents: Audited accounts, tax filings and statutory compliances.
  • • Outstanding questions: Unresolved diligence items and required disclosures.
ILLUSTRATIVE TAKEAWAY

“Acquisition evaluation involves more than reviewing financial figures. Business context, strategic fit and relevant risks also matter.”

CASE / 04 ILLUSTRATIVE FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

Understanding Changes in Business Performance.

A hypothetical business has observed changes in revenue, costs and operating performance. Decision-makers want to understand the factors behind those changes.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

How can financial information help explain changes in business performance?

Diagnostic Framework — Multi-Year Performance Variance
Gross Contribution Operating Margin Cash Conversion
CHALLENGE 01

Revenue Trends

Understanding changes in revenue volume, pricing power and relevant business demand drivers.

CHALLENGE 02

Cost Movements

Considering changes in operating expenses, input cost pressures and fixed overhead structures.

CHALLENGE 03

Profitability

Reviewing gross margins, operating earnings trends and return on invested capital dynamics.

CHALLENGE 04

Financial Assumptions

Understanding and testing the assumptions used in financial planning, budgeting and forecasts.

Primary Analytical Dimensions:

01
Revenue Trends

Contract renewals, customer cohort behaviour and volume elasticity.

02
Gross Margin

Direct labor efficiency, raw material cost tracking and product mix shifts.

Operating Expenses

Fixed overhead leverage, administrative cost inflation and selling expenses.

04
Profitability

EBITDA margin evolution, unit economics and capital expenditure intensity.

05
Cash Flow Dynamics

Working capital absorption, collection cycles and operating cash conversion.

06
Forecast Assumptions

Historical budget achievement tracking and future sensitivity stress testing.
